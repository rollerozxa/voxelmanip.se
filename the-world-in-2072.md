---
title: The World in 2072
---

This text was written in 2022 as part of an assignment in my Environmental studies course in upper secondary school. The purpose of the assignment was to write a text (the type of text was up to us) about a theoretical future of the world and how things would look like in 2072, 50 years from that point.

This text was written around May of 2022, and contains various predictions from the future. Some of it is almost naively utopian (and touch upon a lot of topics that were brought up in the course such as carbon capture technology), while some things reflect the fact that there is still injustice in the world despite all advances that have been made. There is also some commentary in-between the lines about how the all-encompassing Metaverse acts as an escape from reality. People still consume in excess and suffer from health problems as a result of that, but they do so in a way that doesn't harm the planet.

However notably, I did not manage to mention anything relating to AI or LLMs, despite having heard about GPT-3 at this point. Instead I took Facebook going all in on their "Metaverse" concept, which was a topic of discussion (and ridicule) at the time, and interpolated it to the point where it becomes generic, ordinary technology you don't even think about. Who actually owns the Metaverse that is covered in the text is a good question though.

The original text was written in Swedish but has been translated to English with some improvements to clarity. I think this will be interesting as a historical artifact for me to look back at in decades to come.

ROllerozxa, 25th of September 2026

> Author's note: This is a text which describes a theoretical future in the year 2072, written in relation to a young person who has grown up during that time, and tells about what happened prior to 2072 like it were history. One idea which is brought up is the idea of a big virtual Metaverse as a natural interpolation of the development we already see today, with a rapidly increasing technological development of virtual places and Meta (formerly Facebook)'s newly released Metaverse. I have also taken some inspiration from the book Ready Player One, except with a slightly more utopian outlook as the book is actually classified as a dystopian novel.

The world has changed a lot within the last 50 years, but in a lot of ways it remains the same. We are a natural evolution of the information age which came before us, from the early 2000s up to around 2025. But instead of needing to sit down or hold a device to connect, we are simply connected all the time. We may refer the era we are living in now as the Metaverse age. With our Metaglasses, we can both take part of VR and AR experiences depending on what is needed. An AR experience for watching funny KlickNaos on the way to the grocery store, and a VR experience for when you are sitting at home to work.

Previously this was considered something new and novel. The first generation of VR headsets were very primitive, clunky and required to be connected to a device. Not to mention the first generation of online meeting technology that emerged during the COVID crisis in the early 2020s. To think that you need to stare at a screen on a table, where everyone is visible in their own cell on the screen is unfathomable nowadays. Now, you enter a virtual room and sit at a table with other people in the Metaverse. School education is more effective as you do not need to physically transport yourself, and likewise with job meetings and other gatherings. Everything can be arranged virtually in the Metaverse.

It's certainly true that this is a more effective way to meet. In the past, physical transport had to be arranged, with things such as airplanes (air-based transport), trains (rail-based transport) and cars (specifically older models which run on fossile fuels). These still exist today, such as cars powered by electricity, but most physical transport methods have faded out of relevance when people simply do not have a need to transport themselves further than can be done with a car.

This is quite good as most of these transport methods relied on fossile fuels, which was an umbrella term for coal-based fuels that emit greenhouse gases and risked to eradicate the Earth due to rising global temperatures. This was a big concern in the beginning of the 21st century, but thankfully the Metaverse turned out to be a good solution to slow down the rise of CO2 in the atmosphere as it simply made a lot of things that would emit CO2 obsolete and fall out of use.

When you move around, spend time or buy something in the Metaverse, the Earth's resources simply don't need to be exploited to the extent that they do in the physical world. Virtual data is almost endless, it can be shaped and transported through a world encompassing network of fiber cables that guarantee a nice and quick connection of at least 10 Gbps and almost no latency.

But there is of course still a world outside the Metaverse. Despite its focus on the highest possible realism imaginable, it can still not entirely beat the physical world. Many still spend time outside, either with their Metaglasses in AR mode or without them at all, which is common among the older generation that wants to be reminded of a better time, where people wandered the parks with just a device in their hand rather than having it baked into their field of view.

Our foods consist for the most part of plant-based products as well as birds and fish. Other meat such as pork and beef still exists but not to the same extent, since raising them for food is less efficient than adopting a more vegetarian diet. This has made our food industry considerably more efficient, and our more plant-based diet has also led to better overall health than a few decades ago - obesity and obesity-related diseases have fallen significantly.

Consumption in the physical world still exists, but not to the same extent. Most people already get all their entertainment from the Metaverse and feel no real desire to consume in excess when you already have enough food and other necessities.

This stands in stark contrast to the turn of the century and the period that followed, when there was an explosive increase in per capita consumption, to the point where some countries were consuming more than twice as much as the Earth's resources could provide. To solve this, we simply recycled more, created new materials and produced only products that could be easily broken down into their raw materials, essentially making use of everything to create a circular economy.

When e.g. a shirt is thrown away, it gets processed into cotton mass which can be threaded and reshaped into a new piece of clothing. Phosphorus - a substance that is essential for life as it is a cornerstone of all agriculture on Earth - began to be recycled by recovering phosphorus from human waste in sewage systems. This prevented the nightmarish "Peak Phosphorus" scenario which some believed would occur just over fifty years ago.

In regards to carbon dioxide, an extremely large amount was released in the past due to greater reliance on fossil fuels. However, thanks to various CCS (Carbon Capture and Storage) technologies that were developed to become cheaper and more efficient, we were able to halt the rise in carbon dioxide levels in the atmosphere alongside the potential complete elimination of carbon dioxide. Unfortunately this occurred a little too late to halt rising sea levels. One country that served as something of a measurement of sea levels was the Maldives, a country that now exists in name only as the islands making up the Maldives are now completely submerged and uninhabitable.

Unfortunately, there is not all peace and joy for everyone on the planet. Whilst we in the developed world enjoy all this luxury, there are still those who live in poverty. Take Africa and South America for example, which many regard as the world’s power stations and factories due to the vast solar power plants that supply a large proportion of the world’s energy via the global electricity grid, as well as the extremely low-paid labour involved in remanufacturing products from recycled raw materials. It is a grim truth, but also something that many choose to ignore so that they can enjoy a more luxurious everyday life.

Otherwise the world is generally more peaceful for those in the developed world than for 50 years ago. War and conflict has dramatically decreased as countries realised that living in symbiosis solves more problems than constantly infringing on other countries' sovereignty. A major turning point came when the then-People's Republic of China collapsed following a state coup after several years of totalitarian rule and a failure to deliver on promises of economic development and prosperity to the people, which reached a breaking point when the government at the time declared war on the United States in 2037.

Many in China was surprisingly opposed to the war, and this resulted in a coup that overthrew the ruling Chinese Communist Party. Today, China is a democratic republic that cooperates closely with the United States and the rest of the world. Similarly, the former Russian Federation collapsed after a failed war against Ukraine that depleted the country’s resources and isolated it from the rest of the world due to sanctions. It is now part of the Republic of Ukraine, which stretches all the way to the Pacific Ocean.

In summary, we could say that the world is looking pretty good right now, even if it is not perfect. We have overcome big problems that threatened our existence and society has generally been moving in the right direction. On the other hand, mental health issues, for example, remain just as big of a problem as they were 50 years ago - being constantly active and connected in the Metaverse leads to people sleeping less and relaxing less. They are essentially running on full throttle all the time. And of course, the world is still far from being a utopia. For example, people in Africa and South America live in extremely poor conditions while the rest of the world lives in luxury at their expense.
